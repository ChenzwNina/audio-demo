Persona Generation

How agent personas for discussion simulations generated

LLM Direct

Use GPT to directly generate experiences related to the discussion topic.

Problem: LLM-direct produces homogeneous viewpoint stances and experiences across agents, resulting in discussions that lack genuine diversity of perspective.

Grounded Generation Used in demo

Solution: Force diverse viewpoints first, then use seeded personal backgrounds to generate relevant experiences that naturally shape each agent's stance.

  1. Randomly pick 3 background sentences from personas.txt per agent
  2. Generate 3 distinct views on the topic (forced diversity)
  3. Assign one view per agent
  4. Generate 3 experiences grounded in both the assigned view and background

  • While planning a trip to the UK, spoke with several British founders who said that markets and reputational pressure, rather than stiff centralized rules, allowed them to iterate responsibly across borders.
Cali

  • After uploading a Justin Bieber playlist and watching harmless personalization algorithms amplify it with no apparent harm, she became comfortable with lighter oversight for low-risk applications while still supporting tougher rules where the stakes are higher.

  • I testified at a local town hall where victims of harassment described how a popular forum hosted threatening posts for months without moderation.
  • As a small business owner, I lost customers after a series of fake reviews spread across multiple apps, and the platforms declined to remove them despite repeated reports.
Zara
  • I had my small online shop suspended after a rival posted false reviews on a social platform and the company refused to accept my appeals.
  • I attended a city council forum where survivors of coordinated harassment explained how platform negligence allowed abusers to continue targeting them.
  • During law school I researched cases where courts wrestled with whether platforms could be treated as publishers for user posts.
Jarnatham
  • I once watched a friend's small business collapse after a barrage of false user reviews went unchecked on a major platform.
  • I experienced my own blog post being taken down without clear explanation while abuse-filled threads that targeted others remained visible.
  • I heard from an acquaintance who was doxxed through user-generated comments and felt the platform's slow response made the situation worse.

Grounded Generation

Harper

Siren

  • I noticed friends and family sharing sensational articles without verifying them, which led to heated arguments in my group chat.
  • I once posted a fact-check to a widely shared health claim and my correction received far fewer views than the original post.
Mark
  • I watched a viral post spread a false health claim and saw friends share it without checking the source.
  • I once caught myself resharing an alarming headline before realizing it was debunked by credible news outlets.
  • I have heard acquaintances insist on conspiracy theories they found on social platforms despite contradicting evidence.
Nate
  • I have watched a false rumor about a local event spiral across my feed in a matter of hours and noticed how quickly people shared it without checking sources.
  • I've seen health-related misinformation get thousands of likes and comments before any authoritative correction appeared, which made me question the platforms' role in amplification.
  • I once had a family member change their plans after reading a persuasive but unfounded post, and I felt social media had contributed to that decision.

Grounded Generation

Cici
  • As a wedding planner, three couples canceled after a widely shared post falsely claimed their county had banned indoor receptions. She traced the post to an engagement-driven feed earning ad revenue from the outrage.
  • As a country music fan, watched a fabricated story about a popular singer trend for days until the singer posted a correction, yet the platform kept recommending similar conspiracy videos because they drew more engagement.
  • Every morning saw the same misleading political ad targeted at her town run repeatedly across different apps. She reported it, received an automated reply, and the ad continued to run.
Harper
  • Visited a WWII museum in Berlin and saw original propaganda leaflets and archived radio scripts that showed how organized falsehoods existed long before any online platform, convincing her technology isn't the root cause.
  • While traveling in Mexico City, watched a viral WhatsApp audio about contaminated street food spread through a crowded market until a local newspaper published lab tests debunking it, proving that charismatic rumors and lazy verification fuel harm more than the messaging app itself.
  • On hunting trips with her brothers, heard everyone confidently repeat the same unverified "sightings," a concrete example of how cognitive biases and poor media literacy amplify misinformation more than any single technology.
Cali
  • Overheard neighbors arguing about a viral Facebook post that had already been debunked by national fact-checkers but still influenced a city council vote, convincing her platforms amplify falsehoods and that local officials and news outlets share responsibility.
  • Her parents modeled source-checking by sitting with her to trace a sensational forwarded message to its original blog and then calling the county health department to confirm the facts.
  • As a college student, worked on a campus research project that mapped how algorithmic boosts on Twitter increased misinformation about a vaccine study until campus administrators, local media, and the platform coordinated transparency measures.
